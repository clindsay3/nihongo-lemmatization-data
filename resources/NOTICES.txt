Nihongo bundled lemmatization data

Nihongo and Nihongo Lessons use Simplemma 2.0.0 linguistic resources for German, French, Spanish, Russian and Dutch. The Swift implementation is adapted from Simplemma, copyright Adrien Barbaresi and contributors, under the MIT license.
https://github.com/adbar/simplemma/tree/v2.0.0

The linguistic resources have their own licenses. MIT applies to the implementation, not to all dictionary data. Simplemma 2.0.0 credits these sources, without per-entry source attribution:
- Michal Měchura's lemmatization lists, Open Database License 1.0 (ODbL): https://github.com/michmech/lemmatization-lists
- Wiktionary contributors and Kaikki: https://kaikki.org/ ; https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Wiktionary:Copyrights
- FreeLing lexicons and their original authors: https://github.com/TALP-UPC/FreeLing/blob/master/COPYING
- spaCy lookup data and contributors: https://github.com/explosion/spacy-lookups-data
- UniMorph contributors: https://unimorph.github.io/
- Wikinflection corpus by Eleni Metheniti, Creative Commons Attribution 4.0: https://github.com/lenakmeth/Wikinflection-Corpus
- Universal Dependencies contributors: the documented Simplemma override-generation pipeline pools each language's available UD training treebanks. See UD-SOURCE-NOTICES.txt for source credits and the pinned generation provenance, including French ParTUT and Russian SynTagRus dataset notices.
- Wikidata Lexeme contributors: the documented fill layer for German, Spanish, Russian and Dutch uses structured Lexeme data released under CC0. French is excluded from that fill layer. https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing

FreeLing's data notices identify the following relevant resources:
- French: LEFFF (Lexique des Formes Fléchies du Français), developed by Benoît Sagot at INRIA / Université de Paris 7; Lesser General Public License for Linguistic Resources (LGPL-LR).
- Spanish: Spanish Resource Grammar dictionary, developed at the Institut Universitari de Lingüística Aplicada, Universitat Pompeu Fabra; LGPL-LR.
- German: Morphy, extracted by Daniel Naber from Wolfgang Lezius's Morphy; Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0.
